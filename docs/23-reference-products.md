# 23. Reference products

Products to study, what to learn from each, and what to avoid. The founder named Square, Toast and Clover as references for clean merchant dashboards that preview the real product.

**How to use references:** name the principle behind what works, then express it in our own design language. Never copy a layout, visual style, wording or asset. See [21-design-language.md](21-design-language.md) principle 9.

**Freshness:** notes below combine general product knowledge with a light check of public sources in November 2026. Features and prices change. The Research role MUST verify details on the vendors' own pages before they are relied on.

## 4. Patterns to adopt

1. **Self-serve onboarding** that produces something real within minutes.
2. **Edit beside a live preview** of the actual guest experience.
3. **One item library** feeding every channel.
4. **Dashboard home that summarises** the day and points to what needs attention.
5. **Service-style presets** in place of long configuration.
6. **An owner's phone experience** equal to the laptop one.
7. **Ask-your-data** over the store's own numbers.
8. **Guest tab by QR** tied to the same bill staff see.
9. **Curated themes** over free-form styling.
10. **Public, plain pricing.**

## 5. Patterns to reject

1. Hardware lock-in and multi-year contracts.
2. Module sprawl: a new product and a new settings area for each feature.
3. Sales-led onboarding with trainers.
4. Guest-facing fees.
5. Themes built by an internal team for each merchant.
6. Dashboards that describe the product without showing it.
7. Discounts pushed by the platform in the restaurant's name.

## 8. Food ordering study (14 Nov 2026)

A broad study of food ordering products, for principles only. Nothing here is copied from any product, and nothing here changes files 05, 21 or 22. Section 8.8 recommends changes; the owners of those files decide.

**How to read the marks.** **(V)** means a page we opened says it, or we saw it ourselves on a live public page on 14 Nov 2026. **(U)** means unverified: it comes from a search summary, a page that blocked us, a vendor claim, a small student study, or our own inference. Do not build on a (U) line until it is checked in the live product.

**Limits of this study.**

- **Cart bars, item sheets and sold-out flows are thinly documented.** Vendors rarely publish them. Most of the interaction detail below comes from help centres, release notes and a few live web pages. We did not use the apps hands-on.
- **Pages that blocked us:** several Zomato, Swiggy, McDonald's, DoorDash, Starbucks, Mr Yum help and Medium pages returned errors. Baymard's detailed guidelines are paywalled.
- **Phone apps were not tested.** The Research role MUST install the top apps and check every (U) line on a mid-range Android phone before any of this is treated as settled.

### 8.1 Square website themes: how a theme system stays clean

**What the themes page shows (V, live page, 14 Nov 2026).**

- A plain heading, "Choose your theme and start customizing", and one line saying customisable themes come with the Plus plan, from $49 a month. Prices change, so treat the figure as a snapshot.
- A row of filter chips by kind of business: All themes, Retail, Restaurants, Services, Memberships, Classes, Events, Informational. One tap narrows the gallery.
- Cards in a two-column grid. Each card shows the theme on a laptop and a phone side by side, the theme name, the plan it needs, a **Preview** button and a **Subscribe** button. The Restaurants filter returns whole-site designs with a cuisine mood (a Mediterranean one, a fresh-produce one, a dark one for salad bowls).
- The page shows no colour picker and no controls. The merchant chooses by picking a finished design.

**What a merchant can change after choosing (V, Square help centre and blog).**

- **Preset style first, then adjust:** one step applies a whole style (colours, fonts, shapes). The merchant can then edit colours, fonts and shapes.
- **Curated sets:** the blog describes 22 designer site styles and a font list of 100 or more. Buttons (colour, shape, border, shadow), item badges, icon sets, and site width and margin (sliders) are editable.
- **Layouts:** the merchant chooses layouts for item lists and for product detail pages from a short list.
- **Content:** images, text and the arrangement of sections can all change.

**What a merchant cannot do (V).**

- **The checkout page font is fixed** for legibility. Checkout does pick up the site's background, text and button colours and button shape.
- **A customised style cannot be saved as a new template.** Only Square's presets exist as templates.
- **Reset to defaults** is always available. It restores the preset, and if Square has updated the preset since, the merchant gets the latest version.
- One help snippet says a theme cannot be switched after it is chosen. We could not confirm it (U).

**Principles to take.**

- **Start from a finished, art-directed whole.** The merchant picks a design they can already see on a phone. Choice comes before editing.
- **Show the phone.** Every card previews the real guest view as a laptop and phone mock-up.
- **Make the safe path the first path.** Presets come first, edits come second, and a reset is one step away.
- **Lock the money screens.** Checkout type stays fixed while colours and button shape follow the brand. This is the right split for our pay and bill screens.
- **Sort the gallery by business type.** Merchants know what they sell, and few know what "editorial" means.

**What to avoid.**

- **Square still lets the merchant edit most elements** (any colour, spacing, section order). Its cleanliness depends on the presets being good, and bad combinations remain possible. Our file 22 already rejects this; this study supports that choice.
- **A paid plan gate on themes** adds friction for a small restaurant. We have one price.
- **Whole-site themes built for brochure sites** carry hero banners and long scrolling pages. A menu needs a different rhythm: category bar, rows, sheet.

### 8.2 Consumer food ordering apps in India

Two live public pages were read on 14 Nov 2026 (V): a Swiggy restaurant page on the web and the Domino's India web menu. App behaviour is from release notes, listings, press and a few student studies.

**What the Swiggy web menu row shows (V, observed).** One row carries: veg mark, a Bestseller tag, name, price, a rating with count, a description clamped to a few lines with a "more" link, allergen and best-before text inside the description, and an ADD button. After the first tap ADD turns into a minus, a count and a plus. Items with choices carry a small "Customisable" label under the button, so the guest knows a sheet will open. A floating Menu button jumps between categories. A banner states plainly when the address is outside the delivery area, without blocking the page.

**What the Domino's India web menu shows (V, observed).** A rail of 19 categories, many of them deal and offer groups. A "No Onion No Garlic" category sits in the rail, which shows how common that diet need is in India. Cards in a three-column grid carry a tag, name, short description, the size and crust summary ("Medium | New Hand Tossed"), the price and an **Order Now** button that opens the builder. A "Download App" link sits in the header.

| Product | What works (principle) | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zomato** | **All-in price on the menu surface.** A "pay what you see" collection claims the listed price already includes charges and discounts (V that the post says it; a commenter disputes it). **One switch for veg preference** (Veg Mode, V). **One shared cart for a group** by link, each person adds their own (V). Carts kept per restaurant (V, small student study) | Fees that appear only at payment, and credit-only refunds for stock-outs (U) |
| **Swiggy** | **Treat Home to Menu to Cart as a performance budget** for low-end Android: reported cold-start and slow-frame cuts after focused work (V). **Privacy toggle in the cart**, where the decision happens (Incognito, V). **Suggestions with a stated reason** ("What To Eat", V). A web row that carries everything a guest needs to decide (observed) | Clearing the cart when the guest browses another restaurant (V, small student study). Platform and packaging fees (CCPA names drip pricing, V; Swiggy-specific complaints U) |
| **Domino's India** | **One-click reorder** of a past order (V, store listing). **Card shows the build** (size and crust) so Order Now is not a surprise (observed). **A diet category as first-class** ("No Onion No Garlic", observed). A Hindi app since 2019 (V) | Nineteen categories, mostly deals. Coupon-first positioning in the app listing (V that the listing leads with offers) |
| **EatSure** | **A deal builder that warns when a removal breaks the deal**, before checkout (V, release notes). **A shared cart across several restaurants,** each member labels their own items (V) | Heavy first-order discount messaging (V). **India patterns that matter for us.**

- **Fee creep is the main trust problem.** One report puts the final price 25 to 40 percent above the menu price after packaging, platform and surge charges (V that the report says it). India's dark-pattern rules (effective 30 Nov 2023) name drip pricing, basket sneaking, false urgency, nagging and confirm shaming (V). Our no-guest-fee model is an advantage. Say so in the product.
- **Veg works best as a mode.** Zomato made it a switch at the top. Swiggy keeps it inside a filter panel (U).
- **Hindi and speed.** Swiggy announced voice ordering in 11 Indian languages with limited rollout (V). The Swiggy performance work shows where low-end Android phones hurt.
- **Gaps we could not fill** (test hands-on): sheet layouts, cart bar content, in-cart sold-out flows, half and full portion presentation, lite modes, veg mark size and place, ads inside menus.

### 8.5 Answers by topic, across the set

Each topic ends with **Our spec** to show where the base files already agree, so nothing is decided twice.

#### Add to cart: interaction and confirmation

- **Add becomes a quantity stepper in the same place.** Swiggy's web row does it after the first tap (V, observed). Baymard says the same for grocery add buttons and warns that plain text such as "4 in cart" is easy to miss on mobile (V).
- **Items with choices say so before the tap.** Swiggy's "Customisable" label sets the expectation (V, observed). Domino's puts size and crust on the card (V, observed).
- **A quick path to the cart after adding.** Swiggy added it for accessibility so users do not scroll to the bottom (V).
- **No interruption between cart and checkout.** Wolt tested and rejected a modal there (V).
- **Our spec:** instant add, green tick, flight to the cart, stepper (file 05 section 1c). **The study agrees.** No product we found documents a green confirmation or a fly-to-cart, so this is our own signature. Keep it short and keep the reduced-motion path.

#### Sticky cart bar

- **One cart signal only.** A student critique of DoorDash found a floating bar and a top-right icon that mean different things (V, weak source). Zomato keeps a separate persistent cart per restaurant and shows it at the bottom (U, reported in a heuristic review).
- **Toast shows a "Pay my tab" banner once a tab exists** (V). The bar changes its job as the visit moves on.
- **Our spec:** one bar, same size and place on every screen, amber for a stock problem (file 05 section 1c). **The study agrees,** and adds one rule: **no second cart icon anywhere** on a guest screen.

#### Item detail layout

- **Describe the choices on the item page.** Baymard's 2026 benchmark found customisation options often undescribed, which made allergy and restriction needs hard to resolve (V).
- **Give the photo room.** Sweetgreen's teardown asks for more photo space (V). Deliveroo is testing item pages that show ingredients, party size and macros (V).
- **Required choices must not hide optional ones.** A 2026 critique of a US coffee app found that when a required group sits first, guests assume no add-ons exist; the fix is a peek of what follows (V, student critique).
- **Allergen and best-before text live in the description on Swiggy's web row** (V, observed). The text is easy to find and still easy to ignore.
- **Our spec:** hero image, groups in order with a requirement label, sticky footer (file 05 section 4). **Add:** a one-line summary under the name when there are choices, such as "2 choices needed, 3 optional", so the guest sees the whole shape before scrolling.

#### Modifiers and combos

- **Rules per group:** required or optional, single or multiple, min, max, with item-level overrides (Square, V). One required choice shows as a radio list, anything else as checkboxes with a limit (DoorDash, U).
- **Depth is limited elsewhere.** Mr Yum allows one level of nesting, shows it on a separate screen, and blocks Add until a required nested group is chosen (V). Square's nested choices are in beta (V). **Our depth of 2 is deeper than the others.** Keep it only if testing with real Delhi menus shows guests finish in the same time.
- **Structured options replace typed notes.** Petpooja says variations and add-ons let guests tap, so they type fewer notes (V).
- **Deals tell you when a change breaks them.** EatSure warns before checkout when a removal breaks the deal (V). Flipdish leads with bundles (V).
- **Save the build.** Chaayos "Save My Chai" and Starbucks favourites keep the exact configuration (V).
- **Our spec:** one configurator for sizes, add-ons and combos (file 05 section 4). **The study agrees.**

#### Sold-out items and choices, including items already in the cart

- **Evidence is thin on carts.** No primary document we read says what a guest sees when an item in the cart sells out. Toast documents a last-item race it cannot block (V). Square community reports say carts can outlive stock (U).
- **The best flows rebuild the order around what is left.** Deliveroo pre-fills a new basket with surviving items, lets the guest add a substitute, and does not charge the first order (V). Uber Eats gives a timed choice: swap, remove only that item, or cancel, with any price change reviewed first (V). **Both keep the rest of the order intact.**
- **Ask for the fallback early.** Uber Eats lets guests set a per-item fallback before checkout for store orders (V).
- **Two stock states help staff.** Mr Yum separates "Sold Out Today" (resets itself) from "Unavailable" (manual). Square can schedule a reset (V). Staff then do not need to remember to restore items at opening.
- **Choices sell out separately.** Chowbus and Toast allow it (V). Square hides sold-out choices by default and labels nested ones (V).
- **Reorder can bypass the check.** Reports say Starbucks favourites can skip the availability check and McDonald's only flags a combo problem late (U).
- **Our spec:** visible, tagged, never removed silently, named exactly, one-tap fixes, server re-check at send (file 05 section 1c). **The study agrees and our rules are stricter than anything we found.** Two additions: a **"sold out today" state that clears itself at opening time**, and a **test for the race**, where the last portion sells out between Add and Pay.

#### Personalisation and reorder

- **Put the usual on the home screen.** Chipotle shows the regular order with a quick add, and DoorDash puts Order Again on the homepage (V, Baymard). Baymard found reorder badly supported across the set.
- **Keep the exact choices.** Starbucks favourites, Chaayos, and Deliveroo's "retaining modifiers" test (V).
- **Show a reason with a suggestion.** Swiggy's "What To Eat" states why each dish is suggested (V). Ask DoorDash (June 2026) shows a short personal reason per match (V).
- **Make privacy a toggle at the moment it matters.** Swiggy's Incognito sits in the cart (V).
- **Make diet one switch.** Zomato's Veg Mode (V). Domino's lists "No Onion No Garlic" as a category (V, observed).
- **Identity is a phone number and a code.** Toast, Petpooja (V).
- **Our spec:** your usual, last choices preselected, reasons that are true (file 05 section 1a). **The study agrees.** Add one rule: **a reorder always re-checks live stock and shows the result before the guest taps.**

#### First run for merchants

- **State the time.** Bopple says under 15 minutes with a connect, import, test, launch checklist (V). Petpooja says often one day if the menu is clean (V).
- **Fix the menu first, test the whole path.** Petpooja advises clean names, an image for every item, and a test on mobile data only (V).
- **Draft, then Public, with a live phone preview.** Sunday (V).
- **Printed codes must outlive edits.** Toast warns that changing the ordering URL breaks every printed code (V).
- **Avoid vendor-built setup.** Mr Yum via Toast can take up to four weeks (V). Square needs a paid plan, a template and published page before QR works (V).
- **Nothing reliable found** on the first-run idea of generating a menu from a photo. Treat it as ours.
- **Our spec:** menu from a photo, a look proposed from the brand, live preview, publish (files 21 and 22). **The study agrees.** Add: **a stable QR per table that never changes when the menu or look changes,** and **a test order before first publish.**

#### Theming guardrails

- **Square:** presets first, curated sets, reset, fixed checkout font (V, section 8.1).
- **Sunday:** one look per venue, live preview, a nudge toward standard fonts when readability drops (V). Editable: logo, cover, three font roles, colours, banners (V).
- **Toast Mobile Order and Pay:** the setup wizard offers a sign colour and a logo only (V). The separate Online Ordering Pro adds colours, fonts, templates (V).
- **Our spec:** looks, accent, logo, cover, welcome note; contrast enforced; fixed marks stay fixed (file 22). **The study agrees,** and Sunday and Toast show most venues are happy with logo, accent and cover. **Add** a one-tap **"Reset to the look's defaults"** and a rule that **pay, bill and legal screens keep fixed type and spacing** in every look (v1 has one font, so this costs nothing now).

### 8.6 Character assistants and mascots

The founder wants a friendly, human-like waiter in the guest app. File 26 already decides the form ("Captain" is a working name), the dock button, what it knows, honesty and safety. This section adds evidence and rules that file 26 does not yet state.

| Product | Tone and where it appears | How restrained | What annoys |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Duolingo (owl)** | Funny and playful. Appears at emotional peaks: streak milestones where the owl changes form (V), and in reminders (V) | Not capped by any rule we found. Restraint is in the design: simple shape, fixed colours (U) | **Guilt as the engine.** Streak loss plus a sad character reads as pressure. Users joke about a toxic ex (V) |
| **Cleo** (money app) | Two chat modes, hype or roast, chosen by the user (V) | Tone is a user choice | Roast humour about money can sting. Some users find it unfit for serious planning (U) |

| **Domino's Dom** (voice assistant, 2014, later text and voice) | A named voice that guides size, toppings, crust, sides. Customers can skip it and order by hand (V for the 2014 launch) | **Narrow job:** build a standard order | Voice does not suit every setting; open recommendations are harder than a fixed builder (our inference) |
| **Drive-thru voice AI** (Taco Bell, Wendy's, McDonald's, others) | Takes orders by voice | Humans checking in the background | Wrong orders, absurd quantities, orders from another restaurant, and a **hidden human** behind it (V for Taco Bell; U for the rest). Customer-facing failures do the most brand damage (V) |
| **Ask DoorDash** (June 2026) | Text and photo prompts build a cart, with a short reason per match (V) | No persona described | Dietary strictness and error limits not stated (V that they are absent) |
| **Clippy** (Office) | Popped up unasked on common tasks | Hard to turn off | **Interruptions, irrelevant help, and blame drawn onto the character.** The next Office shipped with it off (V) |

**What we did not find.** No dine-in QR product with a well documented AI waiter and real user evidence. Mr Yum has none that we could find (U). There is also no independent data on how often menu assistants invent ingredients (U). Treat this as open ground, pilot it, and measure.

**Placement rules (add to file 26 section 3).**

1. **The first second after the scan belongs to the restaurant.** The character is not on screen at first load. It appears in the dock from the first interaction (file 21 section 10 says the scan should feel like the restaurant welcoming you in).
2. **It never speaks first.** No bubble, no wave, no auto-open. It answers when tapped, or when a guest's own action creates a need (sold-out swap in the cart).
3. **Each placement has one job.** Dock: ask anything. Item sheet: ask about this dish. Compare: explain the difference. Cart: fix a stock problem. Nothing else.
4. **Never on payment, the bill, or any full-screen flow** (already in file 26).

**Manner rules (add to file 26 sections 7 and 9).**

1. **State the narrow job once, at first open:** answer about dishes, compare, suggest a meal, call a person. Dom's narrow job worked (V).
2. **Let the guest switch it off.** A "Hide" control on the chat sheet, remembered on the device. File 26 hides it only when the AI service is down. The Clippy evidence says a visible off switch is the cure for annoyance (V).
3. **An expression budget.** Pleased and Apologetic states appear only after a guest's own action or a real failure, at most once per round. No idle loops after the first few seconds. This copies the reason Duolingo's peaks work, without the pressure.
4. **No messages outside the visit.** The character does not send notifications, WhatsApp messages or "we miss you" lines. No streaks, no counters, no guilt (already in file 26; this makes the channel limit explicit).
5. **A humour budget by moment.** Light humour only in welcome and success. None in allergy, sold-out, error, payment or health moments (Monzo, V). File 26 forbids humour about food safety and health; extend to errors and sold-out.
6. **No tone modes in v1.** Cleo's roast mode is wrong for guests (V that it exists). One calm voice, in the guest's language.
7. **Allergy questions get one fixed answer and a staff call.** The character does not improvise a reply. The venue itself will not promise allergen-free food (Starbucks UK says so, V), so the character must not. Vendor guidance for menu bots says the same, and says staff should receive the context of the chat (V, vendor source).
8. **Say when a human is involved.** "I have asked Ravi to come to your table." Taco Bell's hidden human drew criticism (V). Pass the guest's question to staff so the guest does not repeat it.
9. **Every claim has a reason from the data.** Ask DoorDash shows a reason per match (V). File 26 section 9 already forbids invented facts.
10. **Never pair the character with calorie advice.** It answers a calorie question when asked, in the same neutral tone as any fact, and never praises or warns. See section 8.7.

**Acceptance checks to add to file 26 section 11.**

- **The character never opens itself** in 100 percent of scripted sessions.
- **Hide works and persists** across a rescan on the same device.
- **No allergy answer from the character** in 100 percent of test cases (already in file 26).
- **No outbound message** is ever sent in the character's name.

### 8.7 Nutrition at a glance and compare

File 05 section 1d already decides the row (about 420 kcal and 28 g protein per serving), the "about" rule, the estimate label, and a two-dish compare with a one-line verdict. This section adds evidence.

**How others show numbers (V unless marked).**

- **Starbucks (UK):** values are for the standard recipe with default milk and do not change with customisation. Caffeine is "approximate". A line says adults need around 2,000 kcal a day. Allergen icons appear for food, not drinks. Starbucks states it cannot guarantee any item is allergen free.
- **Sweetgreen:** a real-time macro calculator that shows how swaps change the total (U, press). A teardown found that nutrition values which looked inaccurate cost trust (V).
- **McDonald's and Chipotle:** official calculators that build a total from the chosen items (U, pages blocked). **No chain we found offers a side-by-side compare of two dishes** (U, absence of evidence). The calculators add up a build; none compares two finished dishes. Our compare screen is a differentiator.
- **Swiggy:** a Low Cal view groups dishes in ranges (0 to 300, 300 to 400, 400 to 500 kcal) and a High Protein view shows grams of protein and total calories per dish, minimum 15 g (V). Neither page states a data source or a disclaimer. **Ranges beat a single exact number.**
- **Zomato:** a health score from AI and restaurant data, with the method not explained (V). Commentators say it has flaws. **Avoid an opaque score.**

**India: menu calorie labelling (V, secondary sources; the gazette text itself was not opened).**

- **The rule:** a notification dated 21 Aug 2020 added Rule 2.4.6 to the food labelling regulations. It applies to restaurants with a central licence or outlets in 10 or more locations. Menus, boards or booklets show kcal per serving and the serving size, plus a line that an average active adult needs 2,000 kcal a day and needs vary. Allergen information and the veg or non-veg mark are also required. Declared values may differ by up to 25 percent. E-commerce operators must publish what the establishments provide.
- **Dates conflict.** Sources give a 1 Jan 2022 start, then extensions to June or July 2022. One source adds a turnover condition (25 crore) that others omit. **Check the gazette before any copy cites a threshold.**
- **Enforcement:** notices went to chains in 2022, with reports of licences suspended and "improvement notices" because test parameters were not final. Industry objections: recipes are not standard, regional variety is huge, reprinting menus costs money.
- **Status 2023 to 2026:** we found no amendment, repeal or new draft, and no audit data (U, absence of evidence).
- **What it means for us.** Most independent Delhi restaurants are likely outside the rule (our inference; confirm by outlet count and licence type). Chains inside it need calories, serving size, allergens and the veg mark. Our AI estimate must never be presented as regulatory compliance. An owner-confirmed value is a different thing from an estimate, and the label already says so.

**Do labels change behaviour, and do they harm? (V).**

- **Effect is small.** A meta-analysis of 19 studies found about 18 fewer kcal per meal overall, with the restaurant-setting effect not statistically significant. Treat the row as a convenience for guests who want it. It will not change much behaviour.
- **Harm for some people is real.** A 2025 systematic review (16 studies, about 8,000 participants) found mostly negative effects for people with eating disorders: restaurant avoidance, triggered thoughts, more attention on numbers. No study included young people. Some people find labels useful for safe social eating (U).

**Rules to add to file 05 section 1d.**

1. **Serving size with every kcal figure** ("1 plate, about 350 g"). India's rule requires it for chains (V), and "per serving" alone is vague.
2. **Round to the nearest 10 kcal and 1 g,** since the legal tolerance is 25 percent. "About 420 kcal" should not read "417".
3. **One reference line** in the item sheet and compare footer: "An average adult needs about 2,000 kcal a day. Needs vary." Show it once, small.
4. **Say what the number covers.** When the guest picks choices that do not change the numbers, show "for the standard recipe" (Starbucks does, V).
5. **Compare must not call the lower calorie number the better one.** File 05 says "the better value for each number is quietly highlighted". Highlight only price. Show calories and protein side by side in the same neutral colour, with the verdict line stating the difference as a fact.
6. **Never use red, green, "good", "bad", "light" as praise, goals or streaks** on nutrition. Keep the row small and neutral.
7. **A diner can hide nutrition** with one setting kept in their preferences. When hidden, the row and the compare numbers are absent, and the waiter does not volunteer calories.
8. **No calorie-first sorting by default.** Calorie and protein filters appear only when the guest asks, or through the waiter.
9. **Allergens come from the owner, never from the estimate.** India's rule asks chains for allergen information. That needs an owner-entered allergen field, which sits outside file 05 (see files 15 and 18).
